\begin{abstract}
Counting how often an action repeats needs two abilities: temporal precision at
every tempo, and knowing what one repetition is. Dedicated counters observe a video
at a single temporal sampling rate, although the best rate depends on the tempo of
the repetition; video multimodal LLMs know what is repeated but cannot count. We
present \method, a density counter that a tuned language model refines rather than
replaces. A count-adapted video transformer reads the video at frame strides 2, 4
and 8 and at two depths, and overlapping clips are averaged into token streams on
a shared frame grid. This gives the pyramid an exact property: shifting it by one
level yields the encoder output of the video played twice as fast, a free tempo
augmentation and a second test-time view. One decoder is trained on the union of
RepCount-A, UCFRep and Countix with a dataset token and balanced sampling. A
LoRA-tuned language model then reads a description of the repeated action and the
decoder's temporal tokens and feeds its hidden states back into the density, whose
integral remains the count.
