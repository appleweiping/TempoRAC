\begin{abstract}
Counting how often an action repeats needs two abilities: temporal precision at
every tempo, and knowing what one repetition is. Dedicated counters observe a video
at a single temporal sampling rate, although the best rate depends on the tempo of
the repetition; video multimodal LLMs know what is repeated but cannot count. We
present \method, a density counter that asks a language model only what language
is good at. A count-adapted video transformer reads the video at frame strides 2,
4 and 8 and at two depths, and overlapping clips are averaged into token streams
on a shared frame grid. This gives the pyramid an exact property: shifting it by
one level yields the encoder output of the video played twice as fast, a free
tempo augmentation and a second test-time view. One decoder is trained on the
union of RepCount-A, UCFRep and Countix. The two views are right in different
tempo ranges, and which one to trust depends on the action: a frozen video MLLM
describes what is repeated, and the views are weighted by how they fared on
held-out videos with similar descriptions. The count remains a weighted sum of
density integrals.
