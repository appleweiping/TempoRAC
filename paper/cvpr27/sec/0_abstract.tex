\begin{abstract}
Repetition counting is dominated by tempo: cycles last from a fraction of a second
to several seconds and drift within a video, yet many counters observe every
video at a single temporal sampling rate, and the best rate depends on the tempo of
the repetition. We present \method, which reads a video at several tempi at once.
One frozen video transformer encodes the video at frame strides 2, 4 and 8 and at
two depths; overlapping clips are averaged into token streams aligned on a shared
frame grid, which are fused moment by moment and decoded into an event density
whose sum is the count. Shifting the pyramid reuses coarser cached streams as a
tempo-accelerated training and test view; corresponding unpadded clips have
identical encoder inputs. One decoder is trained
on the union of the public counting datasets with a dataset token and balanced
sampling. On RepCount-A, \method reaches a test MAE of 0.143 over five seeds, the
lowest reported MAE among the listed RGB-based counters under their respective
protocols, and 12\% below the reported value of a counter built on a 7B language
model. This historical result snapshot uses test-informed framework selection and
splits with unresolved source overlaps; training checkpoints use validation.
\end{abstract}
