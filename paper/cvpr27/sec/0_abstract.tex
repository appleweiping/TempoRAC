\begin{abstract}
Repetition counting is dominated by tempo: cycles last from a fifth of a second
to several seconds and drift within a video, yet current counters observe every
video at a single temporal sampling rate. We show that the best sampling rate
depends on the tempo of the repetition, and that the obvious remedy---estimate
the local tempo and route each moment to the matching rate---does not work: tempo
estimated from frozen video features is too unreliable to commit to. We propose
\method, which instead reads the video at several tempi at once. One frozen video
transformer encodes the video at frame strides 2, 4 and 8, overlapping clips are
averaged into token streams aligned on a shared frame grid, and the streams are
fused per moment and decoded once into an event density trained by plain density
regression. A knock-out analysis shows that the trained counter assigns fast
repetitions to fine strides and slow ones to coarse strides on its own.
