\section{TempoRAC}
\label{sec:method}

Given a video of $T$ frames, a counter predicts a non-negative event density
$\hat d(t)$ whose sum is the repetition count, $\hat c=\sum_t\hat d(t)$.
\method (\cref{fig:framework}) is built around one variable, the local tempo,
and uses it at three coupled places: (B) a multi-rate token pyramid gives every
tempo a well-sampled view and produces event evidence; (A) a masked local
tempogram measures, for every moment, at which lags the signal repeats and how
reliably; (C) a tempo-consistent decoder checks the event density against the
tempogram and corrects harmonic over- and under-counting. A and C share the
tempogram, B and C share the density, and all three operate on one time axis.

\subsection{(B) Multi-rate event evidence}
\label{sec:pyramid}
A frozen video transformer $\mathcal{E}$ encodes a 16-frame clip into 8
temporal tokens. For each sampling rate $s\in\mathcal{S}=\{2,4,8\}$ we slide
clips spanning $16s$ frames with a hop of $4s$ frames, so that a token covers
$2s$ frames and every token position is seen by four overlapping clips, whose
tokens we average. This yields token streams
$\mathbf{X}^{(s)}\in\R^{T_s\times P\times C}$ on rate-specific grids of
$T_s=\lceil T/2s\rceil$ cells ($P$ spatial tokens). A fine stream resolves fast
repetitions but sees a short context; a coarse stream contains several slow
cycles per clip (\cref{sec:observation}). Each stream is pooled over space by
attention, upsampled to the finest grid by repetition, and the streams are fused
per cell, $\mathbf{h}_t=W[\mathbf{x}^{(2)}_t\Vert\mathbf{x}^{(4)}_t\Vert
\mathbf{x}^{(8)}_t]$. A temporal encoder (depthwise temporal convolution and
self-attention blocks) turns $\mathbf{h}$ into the event density $d(t)$ through
a softplus head. All rates share the encoder $\mathcal{E}$; they differ only in
temporal sampling.

\subsection{(A) Local masked tempogram}
\label{sec:tempogram}
Let $\mathbf{z}_t$ be the unit-normalised, gradient-stopped features of the
finest stream and $q_t$ its validity mask. For lags $\tau=1,\dots,L$ we compute
the windowed, mask-normalised autocorrelation
\begin{equation}
A(t,\tau)=\frac{\sum_u w(u-t)\,q_u q_{u+\tau}\,\langle\mathbf{z}_u,\mathbf{z}_{u+\tau}\rangle}
{\sum_u w(u-t)\,q_u q_{u+\tau}},
\label{eq:tempogram}
\end{equation}
with a Hann window $w$. $A(t,\cdot)$ peaks at the local period and its multiples,
and the denominator records how much valid evidence supports each lag. We
z-score $A(t,\cdot)$ over supported lags, which removes the generic decay of
feature similarity with lag and keeps the repetition peaks. The tempogram is
parameter-free and costs one Gram matrix and a gather. It also biases
self-attention: head $h$ adds $\alpha_h\,\tilde A(t,|t'-t|)$ to its logits, so
a moment can attend to the moments one or more periods away.

\subsection{(C) Tempo-consistent decoding}
\label{sec:decoder}
The dominant failure of density counters is harmonic: a symmetric motion such as
the down- and up-stroke of a push-up is counted as two events, or two cycles are
merged into one (\cref{sec:analysis}). The density itself proposes a local
period: with $\bar d(t)$ its Hann-smoothed value, $p_d(t)=1/\bar d(t)$ (cells
per event; gradient stopped). If counting is correct, the tempogram should show
its strongest repetition at $p_d$; double counting puts the true repetition at
$2p_d$, merging at $p_d/2$. We read the z-scored tempogram at
$\rho\,p_d(t)$, $\rho\in\{\tfrac12,1,2,3\}$, by linear interpolation along the
lag axis, together with each lag's in-range flag, $\log p_d$ and $\log\bar d$,
and a two-layer MLP $g$ outputs a bounded multiplicative correction
\begin{equation}
\hat d(t)=d(t)\cdot 2^{\tanh g(\cdot)}\in\big[\tfrac12 d(t),\,2d(t)\big],
\qquad \hat c=\sum_t \hat d(t).
\label{eq:decoder}
\end{equation}
The last layer of $g$ is initialised to zero, so decoding starts as the identity.
The correction is applied once to the full-length density; windows are never
counted independently.

\subsection{Training}
Targets are unit-mass Gaussians centred on the annotated cycles, with standard
deviation one quarter of the cycle length, summed into $y(t)$. We minimise two
terms,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\lambda\,\frac{1}{T}\sum_t\big(\hat d(t)-y(t)\big)^2
+\frac{|\hat c-c|}{c+1},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
a density error and a normalised count error, both on the decoded output. No
period, routing or auxiliary loss is used; the tempogram and the decoder are
trained only through \cref{eq:loss}.
