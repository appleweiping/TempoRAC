\section{TempoRAC}
\label{sec:method}

Given a video of $T$ frames, a counter predicts a non-negative event density
$\hat d(t)$ on a fine temporal grid whose sum is the repetition count,
$\hat c=\sum_t\hat d(t)$. \method (\cref{fig:framework}) reads the video at
several tempi at once: a frozen video transformer encodes the video at several
frame strides, the token streams are aligned on a shared frame grid, fused at
every moment, and decoded once into a density. We describe the three parts and
then the objective.

\subsection{Multi-rate token pyramid}
\label{sec:pyramid}
A frozen video transformer $\mathcal{E}$ maps a clip of 16 frames to
$8\times H\times W$ tokens; each temporal token summarises two consecutive input
frames (tubelet size 2). For a frame stride $s$ the clip spans $16s$ frames of
the video and one token covers $2s$ frames. We use strides
$\mathcal{S}=\{2,4,8\}$, so the pyramid's clips span roughly 1, 2 and 4 seconds
at 30\,fps and its tokens cover 4, 8 and 16 frames. The fine level resolves
repetitions as short as a fifth of a second; the coarse level sees two to four
slow cycles inside a single clip (\cref{sec:observation}). All levels use the
same encoder and pre-processing, so they differ only in temporal sampling.

\paragraph{Dense, grid-aligned encoding.}
For each stride we start a clip every $4s$ frames. A clip starting at frame $j$
contributes its $k$-th temporal token to grid cell $j/2s+k$ of a stride-specific
grid with $\lceil T/2s\rceil$ cells of $2s$ frames, so every cell is seen by
four overlapping clips with different temporal context; we average their tokens.
This makes each stream a regular sequence on a known frame grid, which is what
allows streams of different strides to be fused cell by cell, and the averaging
removes the dependence of a token on where its clip happened to start. Spatial
tokens are average-pooled to $4\times4$ and stored, so the encoder runs once per
video and stride.

\subsection{Cross-rate fusion}
Each stream $\mathbf{X}^{(s)}\in\R^{T_s\times 16\times C}$ is reduced over space
by attention pooling---a layer norm, a projection to width $D$, and a softmax
over the 16 spatial positions of a learned scoring MLP---giving
$\mathbf{x}^{(s)}_t\in\R^D$. Streams are brought to the finest grid by
repeating every coarse cell $s/2$ times and fused per cell by a linear map,
$\mathbf{h}_t=W[\mathbf{x}^{(2)}_t\Vert\mathbf{x}^{(4)}_t\Vert\mathbf{x}^{(8)}_t]$.
A temporal encoder of three pre-norm blocks---a depthwise temporal convolution
(kernel 5) for local motion, multi-head self-attention for cycle-to-cycle
comparisons, and an MLP---processes the whole sequence with sinusoidal positions
measured in frames, and a two-layer head with a softplus output gives $\hat d$
on a grid of 2-frame cells. The fused representation leaves to the encoder the
decision of which stride to trust at which moment; \cref{sec:analysis} shows
that it learns a tempo-dependent division of labour.

\subsection{Single decoding}
The density is predicted for the whole video in one pass (the longest RepCount-A
video yields 1{,}320 cells) and summed once, $\hat c=\sum_t\hat d(t)$, rounded
for evaluation. No window is counted on its own, so there is no boundary
reconciliation step.

\subsection{Objective}
For each annotated cycle $[a_k,b_k]$ we place a Gaussian at $(a_k+b_k)/2$ with
standard deviation $(b_k-a_k)/4$, normalised to unit mass on the cell grid
(cycles cut by a training crop keep their partial mass), and sum them into a
target $y(t)$ with $\sum_t y(t)=c$. The only loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{T'}\sum_{t}\big(\hat d(t)-y(t)\big)^2 ,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
averaged over valid cells $T'$. Several counters add a normalised count error
to the density error~\cite{sinha2024escounts}; in our setting this extra term
consistently hurts (\cref{tab:ablation}).
